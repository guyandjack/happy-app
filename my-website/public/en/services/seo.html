Technical SEO

Technical SEO checks that search engines can crawl, understand and index your pages in good conditions. The review covers URLs, redirects, missing pages, canonical tags, the sitemap, robots.txt and relevant structured data.

Corrections are prioritised according to their impact, risk and your site’s technical reality. Performance, mobile experience and Core Web Vitals are measured to identify obstacles to reduce, without promising a particular score or ranking.

Depending on your objectives, support can include analysis of existing backlinks, improvements to content that may be cited, and targeted outreach. No artificial practice, paid link or link acquisition is guaranteed.

Local SEO

Local SEO is for businesses whose customers search for an offer in a specific area. It is relevant when the area served, contact details and local offer are genuine, consistent and useful to potential customers.

The work can cover eligibility for and optimisation of a Google Business Profile, consistency of your name, address and phone number, relevant local pages, reviews and the information shown on your site. It does not mean creating an artificial presence in areas you do not serve.

GEO: Generative Engine Optimization

GEO (Generative Engine Optimization) complements SEO by helping AI-powered search engines and conversational assistants understand your business. The aim is to make your content clear, reliable and easy to verify when these tools synthesise an answer to a question.

GEO relies on precise answers, verifiable information, readable editorial structure, consistent entities and suitable schema.org data. It strengthens SEO without promising a ranking, content reuse or automatic citation by an AI system.

SEO, performance and eco‑design

Eco‑design reduces unnecessary resources while maintaining a useful and accessible experience. It complements SEO by improving the site’s technical and editorial quality, without guaranteeing a ranking increase on its own.

  • Useful journeys and features. Priorities are set around real uses to avoid unnecessary steps and content.
  • Appropriate images and media. Formats, dimensions and loading strategies are adjusted to the browsing context.
  • Controlled code and resources. Scripts, libraries and external services are reviewed to limit what provides no value.
  • Mobile and accessible experience. A stable, readable and usable interface on constrained screens reduces navigation obstacles.
  • Measurement and continuous improvement. Performance is observed in order to target relevant improvements rather than apply generic recipes.

The practices selected depend on the existing site, its uses, budget and project constraints. They contribute to a more sober and resilient online presence; they are neither a certification nor a promise of environmental or SEO results.

Monitoring and continuous improvement

Monitoring checks that the work carried out matches the project’s objectives and identifies points to correct. The indicators selected depend on the scope of support and the access available.

  • Indexation and technical errors. Review of indexable pages, crawl errors, redirects and relevant technical signals.
  • Organic visibility. Analysis of impressions, clicks, queries and visited pages in the available tools, including Google Search Console when access is granted.
  • Priority content and pages. Review of pages that address targeted search intents to decide which updates are useful.
  • Experience and performance. Measurement of load times, visual stability and interactions to identify navigation obstacles.
  • Relevant conversions. Monitoring of contact requests or other useful actions when measurement is configured in a compliant, consent-based way.

Observations are interpreted in context: changes in visibility or traffic may depend on seasonality, competition, search-engine changes and the overall quality of the offer. They help adjust priorities without extrapolating a business result from a single indicator.
