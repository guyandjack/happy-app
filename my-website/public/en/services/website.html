Eco‑design web

Eco-design is considered from the project brief onwards. It means choosing a useful, proportionate response to the need, then limiting the resources required to run it. These criteria guide design and development; their practical application is defined within each project.

  • Start with essential needs Features are prioritised by their real value to users and your business, avoiding unnecessary development.
  • Simplify user journeys Frequent actions, such as requesting a quote or getting in touch, are clear and direct to avoid unnecessary steps.
  • Design for mobile first Small-screen use and constrained connections are considered from the outset, then adapted for larger screens.
  • Choose a restrained, accessible interface The design prioritises legible information, understandable navigation and useful visual elements.
  • Limit animation Animation is used only when it helps understanding, with a preference for lightweight effects that respect user preferences.
  • Choose proportionate technology Architecture, features and dependencies are selected for the actual needs of the project, without over-engineering.
  • Reduce unnecessary resources Libraries, scripts and external resources are limited to what is needed for the expected service and experience.
  • Optimise images Formats, dimensions and compression levels are tailored to the image and its display to limit data transfer.
  • Defer non-priority resources Media and components not visible at first render can load later when this does not degrade the experience.
  • Configure HTTP caching Stable resources can be cached to avoid unnecessary downloads on later visits.

These practices are informed by the RWEB web eco-design best-practice framework. They aim to improve the restraint of the service without promising an identical impact or performance level for every project.

Websites are hosted with PlanetHoster. For its Swiss infrastructure, the host publishes a 100% renewable-energy factor, a PUE of 1.37 and a WUE of 1.22. These indicators describe the host infrastructure; by themselves, they do not measure the overall environmental impact of a web project.

Technical foundations for your visibility

Website development includes foundations that support indexing: clear HTML structure, mobile-ready pages, performance, essential metadata and coherent internal links. These elements help search engines understand the website; they do not guarantee a ranking, traffic or conversion.

FAQ

  • The cost of creating a website depends on its complexity, the features desired and the design. A simple showcase site starts from CHF 1,500,(6 pages) while an e-commerce site or a custom web application represents a more significant investment. A detailed quote is always offered after discussing your specific needs.

  • The number of pages mainly depends on your goals, your business, and your digital strategy.
    For a classic brochure website, 5 to 6 pages are generally recommended: home, services, about, contact, legal notice, and privacy policy. This structure helps organize content clearly and optimize each page for specific keywords.
    There are also one-page websites, where all sections (overview, services, testimonials, contact…) are grouped on a single page. This format is especially well-suited for business launches, marketing campaigns or simple projects.

    From an SEO perspective, a one-page website can be effective for a primary search query, but it is more limited when it comes to ranking for multiple keywords or a wider range of local searches. A multi-page structure generally delivers better SEO performance, because each page can target a specific search intent.
    The choice between a one-page and a multi-page website therefore depends on your strategy: maximum visibility on Google, lead generation, or simply having an online presence.

  • The timeline mainly depends on the project complexity, the required features, and the level of customization you want.
    On average, a professional brochure website takes 4 to 6 weeks, including strategy, design, development, SEO optimization, and testing.
    A e-commerce website typically takes 8 to 12 weeks, due to payment integration, product management, logistics, and security-related requirements.
    A one-page website, especially when built with AI-assisted tools, can be delivered much faster: a few days to 2 weeks, depending on the level of customization and SEO optimization.
    Please note: after purchasing a domain name, DNS propagation (typically 4 to 24 hours) may be required before the website is accessible everywhere.
    Since every project is unique, a detailed and realistic timeline is always provided at the start to ensure a smooth and well-managed development process.
